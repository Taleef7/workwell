## 1. What changed, and why this supersedes 2026-08-04

### 1.1 The engine has a customer, and the customer's measure set is already gated (gated ≠ runnable — stated below)

A primary-care group on MIE's WebChart (the pilot group) is entering a **Medicare Shared Savings Program
(MSSP) ACO** with performance year **PY2027 — measurement begins 2027-01-01**. The ACO's **proposed**
quality set is **APP Plus-shaped** (APM Performance Pathway Plus — the fixed, phased-in set MSSP ACOs
report). Its EMR-reported clinical half, decoded from MIPS quality IDs to the eCQMs they correspond to:

| MIPS ID | Measure | eCQM | WorkWell status (2026-08-30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | Diabetes/Glycemic Status >9% | CMS122 | Vendored, MADiE-gated 55/55, **routed official on TWH** |
| 134 | Depression Screening & Follow-Up | CMS2 | Vendored, gated 36/36, routable |
| 236 | Controlling High Blood Pressure | CMS165 | Vendored, gated 68/68; **not yet routable** (no semantics entry) |
| 112 | Breast Cancer Screening | CMS125 | Vendored, gated 66/66, **routed official on TWH** |
| 113 | Colorectal Cancer Screening | CMS130 | Vendored, gated 64/64; **not yet routable** (no semantics entry) |
| 305 | Initiation & Engagement of SUD Treatment | CMS137 | **Not vendored — and see the caveat below** |

> **Caveat (found in review, 2026-08-30): measure 305's survival is UNCERTAIN.** The CY2027 PFS proposed
> rule (CMS-1848-P, July 2026 — the same rule the standing FHIR-timeline correction tracks) **proposes
> removing Quality IDs 305 and 493 from the APP Plus set beginning PY2027**, which would leave five
> ACO-reported clinical measures. The ACO's proposed list predates or disregards that; whether 305 stays
> in the pilot's final set is an **ACO/final-rule question (final ~Nov 2026), answered before any CMS137
> investment** — see MM-1a and owner step 5.

> **Amendment (2026-08-31, source re-read): 305 has a SECOND reason to fall, and it is the customer's
> own.** The pilot does not run a SUD initiation/engagement measure today and reports little
> substance-use-disorder volume, so it is not a measure their workflow produces data for. That is
> independent of CMS-1848-P: even if the final rule keeps 305 in APP Plus, the pilot has no existing
> practice to compute it from. **MM-1a's expected outcome is therefore "cancelled, not deferred"** — the
> ACO/final-rule confirmation is now a formality to record rather than a fork the plan waits on. Nothing
> is promised on CMS137 either way until it is confirmed (ADR-047's gate precondition is unchanged).

> **Amendment (2026-08-31): MIPS is a SEPARATE reporting obligation, not a naming convention.** The pilot
> must report MIPS **as a group, outside the ACO**, in addition to its APP Plus participation. Two
> consequences the plan did not carry: the MIPS↔CMS crosswalk (MM-0) is tied to a real reporting duty
> rather than being UI polish, and **the pilot's true measure universe is larger than these nine** — see
> the payer-driven item below and §7.10.

> **Amendment (2026-08-31): every EMR-computable measure in this set depends on OUTSIDE data reaching the
> EMR.** All six carry a data source of *external* — HIE records, outside labs, referring specialists —
> and that data **must be documented in the EMR to count toward reporting**. So a patient screened at an
> outside imaging centre is genuinely OVERDUE by the measure until the result is documented, and WorkWell
> reporting them as OVERDUE is correct, not a false positive. This is the same root as the MIE-side
> order/LOINC mapping dependency (§7.1): the gap closes when the result data arrives, never by a
> WorkWell-side override (locked decision 4A.3). Worth stating in the product: the pilot's quality team
> will meet patients they believe are compliant, and the honest answer is "not in the record that counts."

The set's other three members are **not EMR-computed**: two claims-calculated outcome measures
(hospital-wide readmission; admissions for multiple chronic conditions) and CAHPS for MIPS (survey vendor).
No engine work exists for them; at most the Maui dashboard shows them as informational tiles so the quality
team sees the whole set in one place.

**Five of the six computable measures are already vendored, MADiE-gated, and two run CMS's official
artifacts in production.** The 2026-07-24 priority-measure bet (Nicole's list) turns out to have been a bet
on exactly the measures an MSSP ACO demands. That is the position this roadmap spends — with its limits
stated plainly, because **gated ≠ routable ≠ runnable**: the run pipeline derives its runnable set from the
authored measure registry (`run-pipeline.ts` — `RUNNABLE_MEASURE_IDS = Object.keys(MEASURES)`), which
contains authored cms122/cms125; CMS2, CMS130 and CMS165 exist product-side only as Draft/NOT_COMPILED
catalog rows, and CMS130/CMS165 additionally have no `OFFICIAL_MEASURE_SEMANTICS` entry, so routing
construction refuses them today. Making the three run is **official-only measure onboarding — real MM-1
scope** (§5), not a workflow edit. Two more findings from the same review pass: **every vendored artifact's
`effectivePeriod` is 2026-01-01..2026-12-31** and the runtime does not validate it against the evaluation
window, so PY2027 needs a **re-vendor and full re-gate** on the PY2027 artifacts when CMS publishes them
(MM-1d) — stale 2026 logic would otherwise run silently in 2027.

### 1.2 What the customer meeting changed about the product

Three findings from the 2026-08-27 session, each a direction rather than a feature request:

1. **The consumer of the engine is a clinical quality team working provider panels, not another EMR.**
   The quality staff divide gap-chasing work **by provider panel** (each staffer owns specific PCPs'
   patients), explicitly not by measure — one patient may fail five measures and exactly one person should
   own the patient. Everything the roster/case UI grows next follows from that: patient-centric work
   lists, PCP/location filters, saved per-staff filters, and eventually attribution.
2. **A care-gap card must offer resolution, not acknowledgment.** Accept/dismiss is rejected. The right
   actions are (a) *place the order that advances the gap toward closure* — which for simple measures (an
   A1C) is a standing order and for complex ones (colorectal: colonoscopy vs stool-DNA vs FIT; breast:
   laterality/implant variants) is a pick list requiring clinician discretion — and (b) *document why the
   patient should leave the denominator* (exclusion/exception), so the next CQL run excludes them. To be
   exact about (a), because the wording matters clinically: **placing an order never changes compliance** —
   an order is a proposal (`ServiceRequest`, `intent=proposal`, per ADR-067/ADR-013), not a completed
   screening, and the gap closes only when the qualifying *result* data reaches the chart and CQL
   re-evaluates. Both actions fit ADR-067's existing gating (suggestions only on APPROVED terminology
   mappings; no `systemActions`, no `critical`) — the new work is populating mappings and building the
   exception-documentation path, not changing the safety rules.
3. **Orders are inherently local, and that is the integration's hard problem.** A practice's order catalog
   is thousands of locally-named rows (often with billing codes but no LOINC); imaging centers are not
   required to return LOINC on results. MIE's direction: put LOINC/SNOMED on the order rows so measure
   pick lists derive from value-set membership automatically, and let a returning result inherit the LOINC
   from its requisition's order code. That mapping work is MIE-side; WorkWell consumes it. Written
   documentation of the mechanism is a named external dependency (§7).

Two smaller but immediate product changes: the pilot instance is **patient-driven** ("patient," not
"employee," as deployment-level terminology), and the dashboard's status-count chips must **deep-link to
the pre-filtered list they count** — the quality team clicks a number and lands on the patients to work,
never re-filtering.

### 1.3 What is demoted, and what this does NOT change

- **The versioned compliance API loses its "primary contract" framing** (2026-08-04 decision 5). The
  standalone multi-EMR API consumer is not the near-term integration story; the card/CDS surface and the
  Maui instance are. **The API itself stays** — versioned, documented, tested, serving external consumers
  — but no work is justified by "the API contract needs it" any more than by "certification needs it."
  (Precision, from review: the CDS service is a **parallel surface**, not an API consumer — it reads the
  finalized-outcome stores directly, `cds-hooks.ts` → `latestFinalizedByMeasure`. Keeping the API does not
  keep CDS working, and vice versa.) `COMPLIANCE_API.md` remains accurate; only its strategic weight
  changes.
- **M-E1 (occupational content pack) defers.** Locked decision 6 — occupational measures as the long-term
  differentiator — **stands**; the pilot simply proves the engine on a paying-customer-shaped problem
  first. Nothing occupational is deleted.
- **ADR-058's decisions 1–4 stand untouched:** no ONC certification pursuit, the FHIR-column verification
  set stays the bar, no QDM engine and no relabelling, QRDA I/III kept as the interoperability bridge.
- **The packages stand:** `@work-well/measure-engine` / `measure-codegen` remain published; the Maui work
  consumes the same injected-content engine every other surface does.

## 3. North star (revised wording, same star)

**The engine is still the product.** What changes is the proof: through 2026-08, the proof was
measurement against external rulers (MADiE, HL7 validators, cross-engine execution). From 2026-09, the
proof is **a real quality team working its ACO preparation on the engine** — same artifacts, same gates,
plus a user who will find what no harness can. TWH remains the occupational-health showcase; Maui becomes
the primary-care/ACO one. Both are deployments of one codebase. **Stated limit:** these milestones deliver
a **sandbox** (synthetic data, hardcoded accounts). The pilot *running its actual PY2027 year* on WorkWell
— real patient data, PHI — is a separate, later, explicitly gated decision governed by
`docs/PRODUCTION_READINESS_2026-07.md` (the auth fork, tenancy, environment split); nothing in MM-0..MM-4
crosses that line, and no milestone here should be read as authorizing it.

## 4. The measure-set gaps — eyes open

**First gap: official-only onboarding for CMS2, CMS130, CMS165.** Being MADiE-gated is engine evidence,
not product presence (§1.1's gated ≠ routable ≠ runnable). Making these three live means: extending the
run pipeline's runnable-measure derivation beyond the authored registry, catalog activation for
official-only measures (today they are Draft/NOT_COMPILED rows a manual run rejects), owner-reviewed
`OFFICIAL_MEASURE_SEMANTICS` entries for CMS130/CMS165 (routing construction refuses a measure without
one), bundle construction and product surfaces (roster cells, cases, cards) for measures with no authored
counterpart, plus the flip-gate successor below. This is MM-1b's substance.

**Second gap: CMS137** ("Initiation and Engagement of Substance Use Disorder Treatment") — not in the
tree, and not "vendor a seventh measure":

- **Its requirement status is uncertain** (§1.1 caveat): CMS-1848-P proposes removing Quality ID 305 from
  APP Plus for PY2027. **MM-1a's first step is confirmation with the ACO / the final rule** — the spike
  and everything after it proceed only if 305 survives the pilot's final set.
- **It is a multi-rate measure** — two numerators (initiation ≤14 days; engagement within 34 days of
  initiation) over related populations. Everything routed so far is single-rate proportion. The router,
  MeasureReport/QRDA builders, outcome evidence shape, and the roster's one-status-per-subject rule all
  assume one rate. **A spike precedes any promise** (MM-1a): confirm the artifact + MADiE cases exist in
  `cqframework/dqm-content-qicore-2025`, run it through the ADR-047 gate harness, and inventory exactly
  which surfaces break on population-set multiplicity.
- **ADR-047's rule applies unchanged**: no measure routes without a green MADiE gate. Likely capped
  expansions → the credentialed VSAC vendor workflow (the ADR-054 path).
- **No authored counterpart exists**, so `flip-snapshot`'s authored-vs-official comparison cannot run —
  the known wave-2 unroutability wrinkle. The Maui pilot forces the general answer: a flip gate for
  official-only measures built from the MADiE gate + the tenant-data initial-population check
  (`DEPLOY.md` flip checklist), without an authored oracle.
- Two existing debts become customer-facing and rise accordingly: **CMS2's 7 unexplained `NUMER 1→0`
  mismatches** (depression screening is a pilot measure) and **CMS130/CMS165 being unswept**.

  generation by document type, and per-staff card/portlet configuration.

*Verification:* mutation-checked card-action tests in the ADR-067 style (an unapproved mapping renders no
order; an exception writes its structured form + audit event and never touches `outcomes`); for the
unblocked half, a read-model test that a human-closed case is neither hidden nor reported as compliant
while its outcome is still non-compliant.

### MM-4 — encounter-time integration *(blocked on MIE's new-UI source access AND the CDS auth answer)*

- **Real-time ingest**: WebChart pushes (or WorkWell subscribes to) data changes during an encounter so
  cards reflect the visit in progress. Design constraint carried from ADR-067, restated because the
  customer ask ("cards update live in the encounter") superficially conflicts with it: **a card remains a
  rendering of a completed evaluation** — freshness comes from evaluating *sooner* (event-driven
  evaluation on ingest), never from a card invocation triggering evaluation. If that line cannot hold at
  acceptable latency, ADR-067 is revisited explicitly — not eroded.
- **Card embedding in MIE's new UI**: WorkWell cards inside the new WebChart chart experience, via the
  CDS-service surface. Explicit standing instruction from MIE: integration work happens against the
  environment MIE provides, **not** against a live WebChart instance.
- **The CDS authentication gap is a second blocker, already on the record (ADR-067)**: the service
  authenticates with WorkWell's bearer token and **not** the CDS Hooks JWT profile (which forbids
  symmetric algorithms, so HS256 can never satisfy it). Embedding in a real client needs the joint-call
  answer — does WebChart act as a CDS Hooks client, and what are its `iss` and JWKS URL — and this
  milestone must not paper over it with a weaker scheme.
- Performance constraint from the customer, adopted as a requirement: the encounter must not get slower —
  evaluation stays in WorkWell's backend, asynchronous to the clinician's page.
- Scope note: `docs/PROPOSALS_2026-08.md`'s "encounter-close quality check" proposal is adjacent but
  **remains unapproved** — MM-4 is ingest-driven evaluation feeding cards, and nothing here approves or
  schedules that proposal.

*Verification:* the existing CDS service tests extend to the ingest-evaluate-render path; latency budget
asserted; the #470 per-measure-query fix lands with (or before) this milestone since CDS invocation volume
rises.

## 8. Risks

- **CMS137 is double-uncertain**: its requirement may not survive the CY2027 final rule (§1.1 caveat —
  which would *cancel* the largest new-engineering item), and if it does survive, multi-rate support is
  sized only after the MM-1a spike; the honest failure mode is "larger than a milestone," in which case
  the pilot launches on five measures and says so. Neither uncertainty is worth pre-building for.
- **PY2027 timing, twice over**: the pilot's year starts 2027-01-01, so the sandbox must earn trust months
  earlier (cheap-first sequencing is the mitigation) — and **the vendored artifacts are 2026-vintage**
  (`effectivePeriod` 2026-only, unvalidated at runtime), so without MM-1d's re-vendor the engine would
  silently run stale logic into 2027.
- **External-dependency stall** (§7 items 1–2 and 8 have no dates). Mitigation: MM-0's build work, MM-1b's
  onboarding, and MM-2's implementation are unblocked (§7's blocked/unblocked note); the roadmap never
  queues behind a dependency it can route around.
- **Scope gravity from a live customer**: a real user generates requests faster than one developer ships.
  Mitigation: the locked milestone order plus the standing no-silent-scope-change rule; new asks land in
  the backlog, not the current milestone.
- **Naming/privacy drift**: client details leaking into a public repo. Mitigation: the §1 naming policy is
  locked, and source materials live under a gitignored path.
